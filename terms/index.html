2. What the answers mean

For a spot, a firearm and a day, Shootable checks the regulations, bylaws, closures and orders it has mapped, and gives one of three answers:

  • Prohibited: a rule Shootable checks bans that firearm there.
  • No prohibition found: none of the rules Shootable checks bans it there. It is not permission, and it doesn't mean shooting there is safe or allowed by every rule that may apply.
  • Not confirmed: Shootable couldn't settle the question. Treat it as a reason to check with the authority the answer names, or to choose another spot.

Shootable doesn't check everything. It doesn't know about unmapped buildings, people, vehicles, livestock, private agreements, posted signs, temporary closures it hasn't received, or rules it hasn't added yet. Each answer lists the rules it checked and the date of its data. Rules change, and data can be late or wrong.

3. Your responsibilities

Whatever Shootable says, you remain responsible for:

  • holding the licences your firearm and activity require, and following federal, provincial and local firearms law, including the rules on restricted and prohibited firearms;
  • a safe direction of fire, a safe backstop, and identifying your target and what is beyond it;
  • getting permission to be on and shoot on private land, reserves and land under tenure or lease;
  • obeying posted signs, closures, fire orders and directions from officials;
  • checking the official regulation or bylaw when it matters, and contacting the authority when in doubt.

Don't use Shootable while driving, and don't let it distract you while handling a firearm.

4. Safety tools

The downrange check, lane finder, lane cards, 3D views, terrain and backstop checks, danger distances, wind window and road information work from mapped data and models. They know only mapped features and terrain data at about 30 m resolution: a small berm, a cut, trees, a tent, a vehicle or a person don't exist to them. Danger distances are estimates. Forest road kilometres come from the road record and can differ from the signs; the radio channel posted at the road governs. Use these tools to plan, then confirm on the ground. Never rely on them alone.

Alerts and re-checks are best effort. They can be late or missed, and they are not your only source for closures and fire orders.

Privacy policy

Where shooting may be allowed. Never where people go.

Last updated November 10, 2026

Shootable answers questions about places, not people. The rules are on your phone, so checking a spot doesn't send it anywhere. This page explains the few things that do leave your phone, who handles them and what you can ask us to do.

In short: no account, no location tracking, no ads, nothing sold. Saved spots stay on your phone. We count how the app is used (counts and dates, never a place), Google Play and RevenueCat handle subscriptions, and a few optional features ask an outside service for map tiles or a weather forecast.

Who we are

Shootable is made by 10ten Studio Inc., British Columbia, Canada ("we"). This policy covers the Shootable app and getshootable.app. We follow British Columbia's Personal Information Protection Act and Canada's federal privacy law where it applies.

Questions, requests and complaints: hello@10tenstudio.com. That inbox reaches the person responsible for privacy at 10ten Studio.

What stays on your phone

  • Your location. When you tap "Me", the app reads your phone's location to check that spot. It isn't sent to us or stored by us.
  • The spots you check. Answers are worked out on the phone from rule data the app downloads. The spot you check isn't sent to get an answer.
  • Saved spots, lanes and parking spots. Stored in the app only. They aren't uploaded, pooled with other people's or used to rank anything. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data deletes them.
  • Your settings. Firearm choice, map view and similar preferences.

We don't have accounts, and we don't build a profile of you.

The website and early access list

If you join the early access list, we keep your email address and whether you use Android or iPhone. We use them to tell you when Shootable is ready and, if you signed up early, to invite you to test it. We don't share or sell them, and we don't send marketing beyond that. The form is handled by Netlify, which hosts the site. Ask and we'll delete your address.

The website doesn't use advertising or tracking cookies. Netlify keeps standard server logs (such as IP address and pages requested) to run and secure the site.

Service providers and where data is held

We use a small number of providers: Google (Google Play billing), RevenueCat (subscription status and usage counts), Cloudflare (data and map downloads), Netlify (website and early access form), Esri (online satellite imagery) and our weather provider. Some of them store data outside Canada, including in the United States, where it may be accessible to authorities under local law. We only send them what they need for the job above.

We don't sell personal information, and we don't share it for advertising.

How long we keep it

  • Usage counts and subscription records: while the app is installed and your subscription history matters (billing, refunds, restores), and as tax and accounting rules require.
  • Early access list: until launch news has gone out and testing is over, or until you ask us to delete it.
  • Emails you send us: as long as needed to answer or fix what you reported.

Your choices and rights

  • Use Shootable without location: search or tap the map instead. You can turn off location permission in your phone's settings anytime.
  • Turn notifications off in your phone's settings.
  • Delete everything stored in the app by clearing its data or uninstalling it.
  • Ask us what personal information we hold about you, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it, by emailing hello@10tenstudio.com. Because we don't use accounts, we may ask for something that identifies your install or purchase (such as your Google Play order number).
  • If you're not satisfied with our answer, you can contact the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner for British Columbia.

Children

Shootable is meant for adults and licensed firearm users. We don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you think a child has given us information, contact us and we'll delete it.

Security

Rule data is signed so the app can tell it hasn't been altered, connections use HTTPS, and we keep what we hold to the minimum above. No system is perfectly secure, so if something goes wrong that puts your information at risk, we'll tell you and the regulator as the law requires.

Changes

If we change what Shootable collects, we'll update this page and the date at the top, and tell you in the app before the change takes effect if it's significant.

How it answers

Three answers, and the reason for each

Every check ends in one of these, in the app and on the map. Each has its own colour and its own pattern, so you can tell them apart in bright sun or with colour blindness.

Prohibited

A regulation or bylaw bans discharging your firearm at this spot today. The answer names it and the section.

Inside Vancouver, any rifle: City of Vancouver By-law 2280.

No prohibition found

None of the rules Shootable checks bans it here. You still choose a safe backstop and stay clear of people and buildings.

Comes with the rules that apply everywhere, like 100 m from any dwelling.

Not confirmed

Something here can't be settled from the data yet: a boundary that isn't checked yet, or a bylaw that isn't verified yet. Shootable won't guess.

Says exactly what's missing, and who to ask.

No prohibition found isn't a permit. When a source changes and hasn't been checked again, the answer drops to Not confirmed until it is.

Why it's different

Open for hunting isn't open for target shooting

Most BC shooting rules were written with hunters in mind, and many of them exempt a licensed hunter. A target shooter on the same spot gets no exemption.

  • Fraser ValleyNo shooting within 400 m of many roadsThe Closed Areas Regulation closes road corridors to recreational shooting. Shootable shades them on the map.
  • Town bylawsA hunting season doesn't open a townMany towns ban discharge everywhere, or allow it only in mapped zones while hunting. Shootable reads the bylaw and its map.
  • Shot onlyYour firearm changes the answerSome areas allow shotguns with shot but no rifles or slugs. Air guns and bows count as firearms in some bylaws and not in others.

Safety

Safety comes with every answer

Knowing whether a rule bans shooting is half of it. Shootable puts the rest on the same screen, and says Not confirmed instead of guessing.

  • Every answerChecked around your GPS accuracyNear a boundary, Shootable checks a ring as wide as your GPS error. If any part of it falls where shooting is prohibited, the answer is Not confirmed, not a guess about which side you're on.
  • ProLine of fireWhat's in your line of fireThe downrange check lists mapped buildings, roads, trails and property lines out to your cartridge's danger distance. See how it works.
  • ProConditionsDaylight, wind and fire dangerThe day's light, wind, lightning and fire danger for the spot, so you know before you drive out.

The basics still come first. ACTS and PROVE from your firearms safety course, a safe backstop, a clear line of fire, and no shooting toward anything you can't see past.

ProDownrange check

Know what's downrange before you shoot

A spot can be open to shooting and still be the wrong place to shoot toward. The downrange check shows what's mapped in your line of fire, out to the distance your cartridge can carry.

  1. Pick your firearm and cartridge.Shootable uses its danger distance: how far a round can travel if it misses or ricochets. About 1.2 km for a 12-gauge slug, 1.5 to 1.8 km for a .22 LR and 3 to 7 km for most centrefire rifles, a little farther the higher you are.
  2. Point the way you'll shoot.Turn the arc on the map, or face that way with your phone.
  3. See what's in the arc.Buildings, roads, forest roads, trails and property lines, nearest first, each with its distance. Parks and closed areas in the arc are flagged too.

Mapped features only. A tent, a vehicle or a person isn't on any map: confirm your target and what's beyond it every time.

Privacy

Where shooting may be allowed. Never where people go.

Shootable answers questions about places, not people. There are no popular spots, no heat maps and nothing ranked.

The details, including the few things that do leave your phone: privacy policy.

  • Checks run on your phoneThe rules are downloaded to the app, so the spot you check isn't sent anywhere to get an answer. With signal, only the map tiles load from a map server.
  • Saved spots stay on your phoneThey aren't uploaded, pooled with other people's or used to rank anything.
  • No coordinates in analyticsWe count how the app is used, never where.

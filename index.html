When the range isn't enough

Find room to shoot. Know it's allowed and safe.

Ranges are few, booked up and short on distance. BC's Crown land has the room, but no signs: closed areas, town bylaws, parks and fire orders decide where you can shoot, and what's downrange decides whether you should.

Put the crosshair on a spot and pick your firearm. Shootable gives you one answer with the rule behind it, and shows what's in your line of fire.

Open for hunting isn't open for target shooting

Most BC shooting rules were written with hunters in mind, and many of them exempt a licensed hunter. A target shooter on the same spot gets no exemption.

  • Fraser Valley
    No shooting within 400 m of many roadsThe Closed Areas Regulation closes road corridors to shooting, and Shootable draws them on the map.
  • Town bylaws
    A hunting season doesn't open a townMany towns ban discharge everywhere, or allow it only in mapped zones while hunting. Shootable reads the bylaw and its map.
  • Shot only
    Your firearm changes the answerSome areas allow shotguns with shot but no rifles or slugs. Air guns and bows count as firearms in some bylaws and not in others.

What every answer checks

Each check shows where it comes from and how old your copy is.

  • Closed areasAll 221 areas in BC's Closed Areas Regulation (B.C. Reg. 76/84) that restrict shooting: no-shooting areas, shot-only areas, seasonal closures and specified Crown land.
  • Town and regional bylawsEvery municipality and regional district in BC. A bylaw counts only after a person has read its text against the source.
  • Parks and reservesProvincial parks and ecological reserves, and regional parks whose bylaws ban discharge.
  • Recreation sitesBC recreation sites, where firing into the developed area isn't allowed.
  • First Nations reservesReserve land answers Not confirmed, with a note to contact the Nation.
  • Fire and evacuation ordersBC Wildfire Service area restrictions and local evacuation orders close the area. Fire bans are listed, including exploding targets.
  • Rules that apply everywhere100 m from any dwelling, school, church or occupied farm building, and the highway road allowance. Listed on every answer.

Pro · safety

Know what's downrange before you shoot

A spot can be open to shooting and still be the wrong place to shoot toward. The downrange check shows what's mapped in your line of fire, out to the distance your cartridge can carry.

  1. Pick your firearm and cartridge.Shootable uses its danger distance: how far a round can travel if it misses or ricochets. About 1.2 km for a 12-gauge slug, 1.5 to 1.8 km for a .22 LR and 3 to 7 km for most centrefire rifles, a little farther the higher you are.
  2. Point the way you'll shoot.Turn the arc on the map, or face that way with your phone.
  3. See what's in the arc.Buildings, roads, forest roads, trails and property lines, nearest first, each with its distance. Parks and closed areas in the arc are flagged too.

Mapped features only. A tent, a vehicle or a person isn't on any map: confirm your target and what's beyond it every time.

Pro · longer shots

Find a lane, not just a spot

Past 300 m the question changes from "may I shoot here?" to "where can I shoot from here?". The lane finder works outward from the spot you're standing on and shows the bearings worth walking.

  1. Pick your distance band.100 to 300 m, 300 to 600 m, out to 1,500 m.
  2. See the lanes.Bearings where the ground-level line of sight reaches a target in your band, the ground rises behind it, and nothing mapped sits in the fan past it. Bearings with a building, park or closed area in the fan are left out.
  3. Keep the lane.A lane card holds the targets, bearings, ground profile and your parking spot, with the date it was checked. It stays on your phone.

Mapped terrain and features only. Walk the lane, confirm the backstop and what's beyond it.

Pro: plan the day, check downrange, work without signal

The answer is always free, and so is anything that changes it. Pro is for the trip: saving a region for no signal, knowing when the rules change, and checking what's beyond your target.

  • Offline regionsSave a region at home and every rule layer works with no signal.

Where shooting may be allowed. Never where people go.

Shootable answers questions about places, not people. There are no popular spots, no heat maps and nothing ranked.

  • Checks run on your phoneThe rules are downloaded to the app, so the spot you check isn't sent anywhere to get an answer. With signal, only the map tiles load from a map server.
  • Saved spots stay on your phoneThey aren't uploaded, pooled with other people's or used to rank anything.
  • No coordinates in analyticsWe count how the app is used, never where.
